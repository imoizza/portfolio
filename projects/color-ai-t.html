Color-AI-t

A pen-based AI coloring-page generator for tablets, redesigned with a team of five. The idea: make AI one tool on the canvas, alongside the pen, the lasso and a reusable library, instead of a prompt box you wait on.

01 · Overview

AI as a tool, not a vending machine

Coloring-page tools that use AI tend to work in one direction: type a prompt, wait, get a full page, start over if it is wrong. Color-AI-t treats the AI differently. It sits inside a drawing canvas, so you can generate a base image, draw on it, cut pieces out, regenerate a single region, or pull in something you made earlier. The prompt is a starting point, not the whole interaction.

02 · The canvas

The interactions we redesigned

Projects and pages

You name a project and pick a page size, A4 or A5. A plus icon adds a page as a split screen; swiping left moves between pages.

Prompting

A full-screen overlay holds the prompt. You can draw directly inside the prompt area to show the AI what you mean, and switch between prompting a selected region and prompting the whole page.

Local edits

A localized circle tool marks a small area for the AI to change on its own; the test task was adding a chef's hat to a character. A lasso selects a freeform shape, and holding the selection for a moment lifts it off the page.

The library

Anything you draw or lift can go into a library. On any other page you retrieve it and drop it in place, so a character designed once (the study used a cat called Kitty) travels across the whole project.

Toolbar and history

A bottom toolbar changes stroke thickness. A history panel lists previous steps, so you can see how a page reached its current state.

03 · Research

Story interviews first

We ran story interviews to understand how people actually make coloring pages, where AI helped and where it got in the way. The recurring theme: people wanted to stay in control of the drawing and use AI as an assistant for the parts that are tedious to do by hand, not as a replacement for the drawing itself.

04 · The study

Color-AI-t against a baseline

We ran a comparative study, putting Color-AI-t next to a baseline AI coloring tool. Participants did the same tasks in both: generate a character and add a chef's hat with a local edit, then save that character and reuse it on a second page.

What worked

  • Drawing inside the prompt was the favourite interaction, and the clearest advantage over the baseline.
  • The localized circle tool gave enough precision for small, specific edits.
  • Saving a character to the library and dropping it onto a new page felt seamless.
  • Swiping between pages read as natural.

Every participant said Color-AI-t was the easier of the two systems to reach the result they wanted.

What didn't

  • The full-screen AI overlay felt obstructive rather than helpful. It was the lowest-rated part of the flow.
  • Moving between screens and tools that share the same area of the interface was confusing. Participants raised it without being asked.
  • The toggle between selecting a region and selecting the whole page was not obvious.
You need to understand the UI of the application before you can fully utilise it.

05 · What we'd change

Where the findings pointed

  • Rework the AI overlay so prompting does not cover the canvas you are working on.
  • Make navigation explicit between tools and screens, instead of layering them in one space.
  • Act on the top requests: a rough sketch the AI refines into line art, reusing library items straight from a prompt, and guiding lines to control perspective and camera angle.
