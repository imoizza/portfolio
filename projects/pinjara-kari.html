Coursework · The Art of Fabricating Tangible Interfaces

Pinjara Kari

Chinioti latticework, 3D-printed. A 50 cm square of interlocking geometric tiles that flex like fabric, built at the Paris-Saclay FabLab over seven weeks.

01 · The heritage

From a woodcarver's chisel

Chiniot, in Pakistan, is known for Manabat Kari, a hand-carved woodwork tradition. It is what families travel for when they want furniture to last a lifetime. The same geometry runs through Jali lattice screens and mosque facades, and master artisans still cut it entirely by hand.

I wanted to move that craft into digital fabrication without losing what makes it feel handmade. After studying the motifs I settled on one eight-point star as the seed of the pattern.

02 · Digital translation

Figma to Fusion to filament

On a tip from classmates I drew the star in Figma, whose line tools kept the symmetry exact, exported it as SVG, and brought it into Fusion 360 to scale and extrude. PrusaSlicer prepared each file for the Prusa MINI.

The first prints in black PLA came out crisp. They were also too big: the motif broke the 50 mm tile limit, so every pattern had to be rescaled.

03 · The joint

Making plastic behave like cloth

A tile is only half the problem. To turn the pattern into a surface I needed a connector that holds tight and lies flat. Rigid interlocking tabs warped. A pin-and-dot system printed too small. A diamond layout amplified every tolerance error.

The fix came from flexures: thin sections of material that bend instead of snap. By printing the star motif at 0.90 mm for rigidity and the connector bridge at just 0.30 mm, the joint became a living hinge. The tiles fold like a textile and still spring flat.

04 · Material & scale

One colour, two hundred tiles

A two-tone version, red motifs and skin-tone connectors, made the joints the loudest thing on the surface. Monochrome skin-tone did the opposite: it reads as a soft material and lets the shadow of the Kari work carry the pattern.

Then it scaled. Coverage maths pushed the count from 100 tiles to 196, a 14 by 14 grid, printed in batches. For the final run I switched from PLA to skin-tone PETG, for durability and a little more give in the joints.

05 · The installation

A screen that prints light

The finished mesh mounts on a black backdrop on an easel, which throws the negative space of the pattern into relief. Held against a window it works as a Jali screen: the 0.90 mm tiles filter sunlight into a moving field of geometric shadow.
